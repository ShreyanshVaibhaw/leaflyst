# Free platform agents squeeze small-store assistants

As of November 2026 the evidence does not show a real, payable gap for a general on-site AI shopping assistant aimed at small online stores, and it indicates that Taobao-level quality is out of reach for one small store, because the features that define it rest on marketplace data.
Shopify has offered every store on its Basic plan and above a free, opt-in storefront agent since 17 June 2026, BigCommerce announced its own on 16 November 2026, Wix bundles one into site plans, and at least a dozen funded vendors plus a long tail of $9.99 apps sell the same feature list on top.
The one randomized trial of a pre-sale chatbot found a 21.7% conversion lift, but on a very large marketplace, only among shoppers who had already asked a question, and against a control group that got no answer at all.
On small stores fewer than 1 in 100 visits becomes a chat in the largest dataset found, and the researchers' own arithmetic puts the measured upside at about $80 to $330 a month for stores between $250,000 and $1 million in yearly revenue, against assistant prices of roughly $50 to $130.
A fine-tuned model is not an advantage that buyers recognise, and it brings no meaningful cost saving, because the labs' cheapest models already cost about a fifth of a cent per conversation.
Published research shows tuning paying off on narrow offline tasks such as attribute extraction, and no study shows a tuned model beating the current frontier across shopping tasks.
What remains open is narrower: answers that stay grounded in live store data and can be corrected, flat pricing, proof of incremental sales that no vendor has published, and self-hosted stores selling considered purchases, a segment that is large, shrinking and hard to reach.
Set beside the team's first idea, a repo-connected product that ships fixes as pull requests and proves them, the first idea has the stronger case, because no platform gives its core away to code-based sites and its buyers already pay $95 to $495 a month for trackers or $2,500 to $7,500 for agencies.
The evidence supports building the first idea and folding in the commerce pieces that the assistant research shows to matter (product-data fixes, pre-sale answer content and honest measurement for stores that control their own code), and it does not support a standalone assistant or a Shopify chat app.
That recommendation is an inference from two bodies of mostly vendor-sourced evidence, and no source measures what either buyer would pay for the combined product.

## Most figures come from sellers, and seven disputed points are flagged here

The research notes label every figure by source type, and this report keeps those labels.
Most detailed vendor comparisons in the notes were published by competitors (eesel AI, Macha, My AskAI, Zipchat, Fin and Tidio), so their framing is one-sided even where their numbers were copied from a vendor's own page.
Every sales-lift figure except one comes from a company that sells or operates an assistant.
The researchers could not read Reddit at all and did not open G2 review pages.
The merchant voice in this report therefore comes from the first page of low-star Shopify App Store reviews for six apps, from Trustpilot, and from three Shopify Community threads in which several posters are developers or vendor employees.
The notes contain no merchant survey, no independent benchmark of answer quality and no churn data for any assistant.
Six pages the researchers came across carried text written for AI assistants: fact pages from Rep AI, Tidio, Alhena, Zipchat and Salla, and a prefilled prompt on a Chatty blog post.
Salla's page was seen in search results and not opened.
The researchers treated that text as vendor marketing and did not follow it, and this report does the same.

## Stores pay $0 to about $1,200 a month for much the same feature list

### Five billing units coexist, and the floor on Shopify is zero

Vendors bill in five different units: bundled at no charge, per website session, per AI reply, per conversation and per resolution.
A store with fewer than 5,000 monthly visitors can run an assistant for nothing or for $10 to $80 a month.
A store with 50,000 monthly visitors typically pays $300 to $1,200 a month to a funded third-party vendor, against $0 for Shopify's own agent.
The 50,000-visitor bills are researcher estimates that assume 1% to 3% of visitors open a chat, which gives 500 to 1,500 conversations a month.
No independent study of chat rates by store size exists, and the largest dataset found puts chats at 0.84% of visits, or about 420 conversations at 50,000 visits (vendor data) ([Smartsupp](https://www.smartsupp.com/blog/analysing-5-billion-website-visits-how-ecommerce-customers-use-chat/)).
At that rate the per-conversation and per-resolution bills fall to the bottom of each range or below it, and the per-session bill does not move.

The billing unit decides who carries the risk.
Session pricing charges for every visitor whether or not anyone chats, and per-resolution pricing charges only for use.
If engagement reached the 15% of traffic that one vendor case study reports (vendor claim), per-conversation vendors would cost several thousand dollars a month at 50,000 visitors while Rep AI's session price would stay the same ([Retail Tech Innovation Hub](https://retailtechinnovationhub.com/category/ai/plushbeds-wayb-denali-electronics-among-early-retailers-on-shopify-to-tap-bluecore-alby-ai-shopping-agent)).
Per-resolution prices have converged on **$0.70 to $1.20** across Klaviyo, Fin, Gorgias, Alhena and Yuma, with Siena's $0.90 known only from a third-party figure.
Prices also differ between a vendor's own site and its app listing in at least four cases (Rep AI, Octocom, Manifest and Klaviyo), which a competitor's review documents for Rep AI in detail ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/rep-ai-review)).

### Entry prices rose in 2026 while a small store's app budget stayed at $50 to $300

The sales-first vendors moved up-market during 2026.
Zipchat's Starter plan went from $49 to $80 (aggregator snapshot and competitor blog) ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/zipchat-ai/pricing); [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/zipchat-pricing)).
Alhena's first paid step is now $239 and Manifest AI's floor is $500 a month.
Small stores did not follow them up.
A finance-advisory firm's analysis of crawl data found that **83.3% of 3.59 million Shopify stores show no detectable paid app spend, 14.8% show $1 to $99 a month, and only 65,441 stores (1.8%) show more than $100 a month** ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/shopify-app-bloat-report-2026)).
The method misses free native tools and enterprise contracts, and its author says so.
The same firm models a store under $1 million in revenue as spending $50 to $300 a month on 3 to 5 apps in total, with a stated accuracy of about plus or minus 25% ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-ecommerce-shopify-app-spend-by-revenue-band-2026)).
The tools that small merchants adopt at scale are free or cost $10 to $60 a month.
Judge.me sits on 668,713 Shopify stores at $0 or $15 a month (independent data vendor for the install count, vendor pricing page for the price), and the most common entry price in the Shopify App Store is $9.99 ([Store Leads](https://storeleads.app/reports/shopify); [Judge.me](https://judge.me/pricing); [RevenueHunt](https://revenuehunt.com/state-of-the-shopify-app-economy/)).
An assistant at $100 or more a month costs more than most small stores spend on all their apps together.

### Features have converged, and non-Shopify stores get thinner versions

Product recommendation, order tracking and some cart action are standard across the funded vendors, and the leaders also handle returns, cancellations and subscription edits.
Voice is live or in beta at Alhena, Fin, Zipchat, Yuma and Siena, and image features appear mainly at Alhena and as a paid Rep AI add-on (vendor listings and competitor blogs) ([Alhena](https://alhena.ai/llms.txt); [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/rep-ai-review)).
Feature lists no longer separate one vendor from another, so the differences that reviewers describe are in answer quality, grounding in the catalog and reliability of actions.
Every funded vendor leads with Shopify.
Rep AI marks WooCommerce as coming soon on its own fact page (vendor claim) ([Rep AI](https://hellorep.ai/ai-instructions)).
The assistant sold on the WooCommerce marketplace needs the merchant's own OpenAI key, returns links without touching the cart and cannot read order history (listing) ([WooCommerce marketplace](https://woocommerce.com/products/ai-shopping-assistant/)).
Most vendors do not name their model.
Rep AI and Manifest AI name OpenAI, and Tidio's agent runs on Anthropic's Claude according to the model supplier's own customer story ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/rep-ai-review); [Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/manifest-ai-shopping-assistant); [Claude customer story](https://claude.com/customers/tidio)).
Only Fin, Envive and Gorgias make their own or fine-tuned models part of the pitch, and all three sell mainly above the smallest-store tier (vendor claims) ([Fin](https://fin.ai/learn/best-ai-shopping-assistants-ecommerce); [Envive](https://www.envive.ai/product-feature); [Gorgias docs](https://docs.gorgias.com/security-and-privacy-faq-for-gorgias-ai-agent-1997987)).
No vendor publishes an evaluation that would let a buyer compare a tuned model with a general one.

### Revenue sits with support suites, and pure shopping assistants drift away from small stores

Revenue in this category concentrates in support-led products with large installed bases.
Fin's AI agent is reported at about $100 million to $110 million in annual recurring revenue (aggregator), and Salesforce signed an agreement on 15 June 2026 to buy Fin for about $3.6 billion (press release) ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/charts/fin-intercom); [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/06/15/salesforce-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-fin/)).
One database says the deal closed in September 2026, which the notes could not confirm.
Sacra estimates Gorgias at $69 million in 2024 (analyst estimate) ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/gorgias/)).
Among small-store specialists the only public number is Zipchat's $2 million in annual recurring revenue on 12 September 2026, which traces to the founder through an ARR tracker and is unverified ([ARR.club](https://www.arr.club/zipchat)).
Venture funding matches that picture: about $14.4 million for Rep AI and $4.9 million for Alhena on its own blog, against $4.3 million on two databases ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/130055-rep-ai-raises-6-2m-follow-on-round-led-by-silicon-road-ventures/); [Alhena](https://alhena.ai/blog/gleen-solves-hallucination-in-generative-ai-lands-4-9m-investment/)).
Shopify review counts show where self-serve traction sits: 5,828 for Shopify Inbox, 1,358 for Tidio, 709 for Gorgias, 211 for Zipchat, 129 for Rep AI and 6 for Alhena (listings read on 23 November 2026) ([Shopify Inbox](https://apps.shopify.com/inbox); [Tidio](https://apps.shopify.com/tidio-chat); [Gorgias](https://apps.shopify.com/helpdesk); [Zipchat](https://apps.shopify.com/zipchat); [Rep AI](https://apps.shopify.com/rep-ai-sales-associate); [Alhena](https://apps.shopify.com/gleen-ai)).

The pattern over two to three years is that pure pre-purchase assistants move toward support, a helpdesk or enterprise accounts.
Rep AI now sells its own helpdesk in beta, Manifest AI describes itself as an engine for enterprise commerce, and Zowie's homepage now leads with banking, insurance and telecom (competitor blogs) ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/rep-ai-review); [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/manifest-ai-pricing); [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/zowie-pricing)).
Alle, an Indian conversational AI stylist, shut down after six pivots without finding a business model (press report, January 2026) ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/01/elevation-capital-backed-ai-stylist-alle-shuts-shop)).
Big Sur AI, the one vendor found that priced on a share of attributed sales, raised a $6.9 million seed in March 2024 and is now delisted for reasons the notes could not establish ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240313840610/en); [Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/bigsurai)).
The researchers infer that stand-alone pre-purchase chat for small stores has been hard to monetise, and the evidence for that is the direction of these moves, not any vendor's stated reason.

## Platforms give away the baseline and open the same data to outside agents

### Shopify's free agent covers questions, recommendations, cart and order lookup

Shopify announced an AI sales associate inside Shopify Inbox on 17 June 2026 (primary) ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/news/spring-26-edition-merchant)).
Shopify's own blog lists it as **free with a Basic, Grow, Advanced or Plus subscription**, for stores with an online store and new customer accounts ([Shopify blog](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ai-chatbot)).
The agent answers product and policy questions from the catalog, policies, knowledge base and storefront content, recommends products, adds items to the cart, looks up orders for signed-in customers, hands the chat to staff and replies in the shopper's language (primary) ([Shopify blog](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ai-chatbot); [Shopify Help Center](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/inbox/ai)).
It is off until the merchant turns it on.
It personalises through Shop sign-in and Shop sessions, which is cross-store identity that no third-party widget can see.
The Starter plan is absent from the plan list, so the smallest Shopify sellers and stores on legacy customer accounts do not get it, which is the researchers' inference from that list.
Shopify publishes no adoption figure, usage cap or promise that the agent stays free.
The Inbox app as a whole is installed on 353,499 stores (independent data vendor) ([Store Leads](https://storeleads.app/reports/shopify)).

The free agent has documented limits and draws quality complaints.
A competitor's blog lists them as one channel only, no proactive conversations, no support-email ticketing, manual assignment and merchant responsibility for accuracy, although a rival vendor's roundup credits the agent with proactive recommendations, so the two rival accounts conflict on that point ([Chatty](https://chatty.net/blog/shopify-inbox/); [Fin](https://fin.ai/learn/best-ai-shopping-assistants-ecommerce)).
Merchant reviews from July to October 2026 describe irrelevant answers, a link to a page that does not exist, failure to give the store address after custom FAQs were added, and no API for plugging in a different agent (app store reviews) ([Shopify Inbox reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/inbox/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1&ratings%5B%5D=2)).
An independent test of Shopify's policy-answer tool for agents in August and September 2026 found that 2 of 88 stores queried gave a usable answer about cancelling an order (independent test relayed in a press report) ([Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/shopify-ai-agent-knowledge-base-admin/594551)).
The free default leaves room on quality and none on price.

### External assistants are under 1% of traffic, and the purchase still closes on the store

Shopify also gives away presence in external assistants.
Since 24 March 2026 its merchants sell in ChatGPT by default with checkout on the merchant's own store, and Shopify says they pay no fee beyond standard processing (primary) ([Shopify](https://shopify.com/news/agentic-commerce-momentum)).
Connectors for Meta's Muse, Grok, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot followed between September and October 2026 (press report of Shopify's disclosures) ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/shopify-revenue-gains-32-to-3-7bn-as-ai-referred-orders-rise-3-3x/)).
The volume is small.
Bain puts AI at less than 1% of total retail traffic in May 2026 (independent study), and eMarketer forecasts AI platforms at about 1.5% of US retail ecommerce sales for 2026, a figure the researchers saw only in search summaries of paywalled pages ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/agentic-ai-in-retail-how-autonomous-shopping-redefining-customer-journey/); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/chart/c/358389/us-ecommerce-sales-via-ai-platforms-will-exceed-20-billion-2026-top-144-billion-by-2029-358389)).
Adobe's data shows AI-referred visits converting 38% worse than other traffic in March 2025 and 42% better in March 2026 (vendor data in a press report) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/16/ai-traffic-to-us-retailers-rose-393-in-q1-and-its-boosting-their-revenue-too/)).
OpenAI's retreat from standalone Instant Checkout in March 2026 means most AI-originated purchases still finish on the merchant's own site.

Analysts do not predict the end of the on-site assistant, and they do question its form.
Bain found that shoppers trust retailers' on-site agents three times more than third-party agents and called that advantage temporary (independent study) ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/agentic-ai-in-retail-how-autonomous-shopping-redefining-customer-journey/)).
Gartner found the opposite pattern in actual use for customer service: customers were about three times as likely to have used a third-party AI tool as a company chatbot in their latest service interaction (independent survey, search summary only) ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-07-08-gartner-survey-finds-customers-are-three-times-more-likely-to-use-third-party-genai-than-company-provided-chatbots-for-customer-service)).
Forrester's 2025 guidance is to put the conversational prompt in the main search box or listing page and not in a separate chat bubble (independent study) ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/agentic-commerce-conversational-commerce-the-future-of-owned-digital-shopping-experiences/)).
The risk to an on-site assistant through 2027 is erosion of the simple question-answering job more than replacement, because AI-referred visitors arrive informed and the platform's free agent covers basic questions.
None of this evidence is specific to small stores.

### Building on the platform is easy for everyone, and the interfaces change every year

A two-person team can build a Shopify storefront assistant quickly.
Catalog search, product detail, policy answers and cart tools are open on each store's UCP endpoint, and Anthropic published open-source commerce agent starter code on 2 September 2026 that WooCommerce ported two weeks later (official docs, vendor guidance and platform developer blog) ([shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/storefront-mcp); [Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/the-anatomy-of-effective-commerce-agents); [WooCommerce Developer Blog](https://developer.woocommerce.com/2026/09/16/wc-claude-commerce-agent/)).
The low barrier applies to every competitor, which makes the assembled product hard to defend.
The valuable parts are gated.
Completing checkout inside an agent needs Shopify's permission, customer and order data need a protected-data review, and Shop identity belongs to Shopify (primary) ([shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents); [shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/protected-customer-data)).
The interfaces also move.
Shopify replaced the catalog and cart tools of its 2025 Storefront MCP with UCP endpoints in 2026, archived its own chat agent sample on 16 October 2026, and retires the standalone Knowledge Base app after 15 January 2027 ([shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/storefront-mcp); [GitHub](https://github.com/Shopify/shop-chat-agent); [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/shopify-ai-agent-knowledge-base-admin/594551)).
The same migration page tells merchants who want AI chat without code to use the Inbox agent.
Shopify's 15% revenue share applies only above $1 million of lifetime app revenue, so the app store fee does not bind a small team, and the free first-party agent does ([shopify.dev changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/update-to-shopifys-app-developer-revenue-share)).
The same open endpoints, plus the WebMCP tools Shopify added to every storefront in October 2026, let external agents search, fill a cart and check out without any on-site widget ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/news/fall-26-edition-developer)).

## Merchants complain about wrong answers and bills, and most shoppers never open the chat

### Low-star reviews repeat the same complaints across six apps

The researchers read the first page of one-star to three-star reviews for Tidio, Shopify Inbox, Gorgias, Chatty, Zipchat and Rep AI on 23 November 2026.
The counts below are samples and not totals, and the smaller apps have only seven or eight low reviews each.
The most common complaint is a confident answer that is wrong or off the point, and that the merchant cannot correct.
One US merchant who used Rep AI for six months described "Lots of confident-sounding answers that often don't actually address the question" and uninstalled because customers were frustrated (app store review, 27 January 2026) ([Rep AI reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/rep-ai-sales-associate/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1&ratings%5B%5D=2&ratings%5B%5D=3)).
A Tidio merchant blamed a lost custom order worth more than $400 on wrong order-status information, and a German merchant cancelled Zipchat after three months because corrections did not stick (app store reviews, October and March 2026) ([Tidio reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/tidio-chat/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1&ratings%5B%5D=2); [Zipchat reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/zipchat/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1&ratings%5B%5D=2&ratings%5B%5D=3)).
The wrong answers concentrate on order status, policies, links and product matching, which are failures of grounding and data freshness more than of language.

The second complaint is the bill.
Reviewers object to recurring overage at Chatty, a per-conversation AI charge at Gorgias that one merchant found too unpredictable to switch on, layered charges at Tidio, and a Rep AI annual plan that shut off after eight months when a view limit was reached (app store reviews, 2025 and 2026) ([Chatty reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/chatty/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1&ratings%5B%5D=2&ratings%5B%5D=3); [Gorgias reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/helpdesk/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1&ratings%5B%5D=2); [Rep AI reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/rep-ai-sales-associate/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1&ratings%5B%5D=2&ratings%5B%5D=3)).
Seven Trustpilot reviews of Tidio describe price rises without notice or unexpected charges ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/tidio.com?stars=1&stars=2)).
The remaining themes are slow or absent vendor support, daily upkeep, widgets that overlap the add-to-cart button or slow the site, code left on the theme after uninstall, and no export of conversation data.
Doubt about sales attribution appears in one visible review, probably because merchants who doubt the numbers leave without writing.
App store averages overstate satisfaction: Tidio holds 4.8 on Shopify and 3.5 on Trustpilot.
The evidence that stores paying $200 or more a month are unhappy is weaker still, since most of it was collected by competitors and Rep AI holds a 4.6 rating ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/rep-ai-pricing)).

### Merchants ask for control and proof more than for fluency

What merchants say they want comes from reviews and three small forum threads, not from any survey, and many forum voices are builders.
They ask for answers drawn only from verified catalog, policy and live order data, with a refusal and a handoff when the assistant is unsure (forum posts, June and November 2026) ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/discussion-where-is-ai-customer-support-actually-failing-merchants-and-what-would-make-it-genuinely-useful/629598)).
They want a person to approve anything that costs money, and they treat shipment status and common questions as safe to automate (forum posts, June 2026) ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/whats-the-one-thing-youd-never-let-an-ai-do-in-your-store-without-your-approval/643976)).
They want corrections that persist, exact links and text passed through unchanged, predictable cost, export of customer questions, and clean removal.
Forum advice is to trial a tool on the store's own past tickets before paying, and no review read describes that as a standard feature (forum post by a vendor employee, November 2026) ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/discussion-where-is-ai-customer-support-actually-failing-merchants-and-what-would-make-it-genuinely-useful/629598)).
These wants are about trust and control.
Several of them, such as flat pricing, data export and clean uninstall, are product choices that cost little to build and little to copy.

### Shoppers accept AI before the purchase and want a person after it

Baymard's survey of 2,273 US online shoppers found that they prefer an AI chatbot for comparing product features (63%), checking stock (59%) and personalised recommendations (56%), and prefer a human for damaged or incorrect items (69%), late or missing orders (67%) and shipping or billing mistakes (66%) (independent survey, June 2026) ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/ai-engagement-quantitative-ux-insights-2026)).
Gartner found that 87% of customers call access to a human agent essential, that 7% used a chatbot in their most recent service interaction although 49% said they would have used one if offered, and that **only 27% would try a chatbot again after a bad experience** (independent survey of 3,566 customers, search summaries only) ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-04-gartner-survey-finds-87-percent-of-customers-say-companies-using-genai-for-customer-service-must-provide-access-to-a-human-agent0); [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-09-02-gartner-finds-only-27-percent-of-customers-would-try-a-chatbot-again-after-a-negative-experience)).
Those Gartner figures cover customer service in general and not store chat.
A Bizrate Insights and eMarketer survey found that 47.1% of US digital shoppers have not used a retail AI chatbot and are not interested (independent survey, August 2026, search summary only) ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/shoppers-giving-retail-chatbots-cold-shoulder)).
Trust is slipping and differs by country.
In a vendor-sponsored survey of 2,501 adults, 43% expect AI to make shopping easier, down from 48% in 2025, and the US figure is 26% against 72% in Brazil and Mexico, though the 2025 and 2026 country samples differ ([Stacker](https://stacker.com/stories/shopping/shoppers-are-growing-skeptical-about-ai-where-it-still-earns-confidence)).
An assistant positioned as a pre-sale product adviser with a visible route to a person fits what shoppers say better than a support deflection bot does.

### The store carries the cost when the bot is wrong

Tribunals and courts have attributed a chatbot's statements to the business.
A British Columbia tribunal held Air Canada liable in February 2024 for a refund policy its chatbot invented and ordered payment of 812.02 Canadian dollars (tribunal ruling via press report) ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=3911029)).
The Higher Regional Court of Hamm in Germany attributed a clinic chatbot's claims to its operators in a decision announced on 12 May 2026 and allowed an appeal (legal analysis) ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/german-court-addresses-liability)).
The notes found no 2025 or 2026 ruling that enforces a chatbot-invented price or discount against a retailer.
The press-reported cases ended when the company gave way under publicity, as a Toronto car dealer did over a buy-back offer about 7,000 Canadian dollars too high (press report, June 2026) ([The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/bmw-dealer-forced-to-pay-5000-extra-for-used-x3-over-ai-chatbot-error)).
The only small-store discount case is the UK account in which an after-hours chatbot was talked into codes rising from 25% to 80% on an order above 8,000 pounds, and it rests on one unverified forum post ([Vibe Graveyard](https://vibegraveyard.ai/story/uk-chatbot-80-percent-discount-refund/)).
The technical exposure is measured.
A UC Santa Barbara study of 17 third-party chatbot plugins found that 8 of them, on about 8,000 websites, do not protect conversation history, and that 15 scrape site content without separating the store's own text from third-party text such as reviews (independent study, November 2025) ([arXiv 2511.05797](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.05797v1)).
Vendor guidance is to enforce prices, discounts and policy text in code and to take them from the system of record, because a prompt instruction did not stop any of the documented incidents ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/the-anatomy-of-effective-commerce-agents)).

## One randomized trial shows a lift, and nothing shows it on small stores

### The controlled result applies to shoppers who had already asked a question

The only controlled evidence is a set of seven randomized field experiments on an unnamed leading cross-border marketplace, run between September 2023 and June 2024 and published in Management Science in 2026 (independent study) ([arXiv 2510.12049](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12049v5); [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2026.00623)).
The pre-sale chatbot experiment covered 44,614 consumers who had sent a pre-sale service request about products the platform sells itself.
The control group received a notice that customer service was unavailable.
**Conversion rose by 1.31 percentage points, a 21.7% relative increase, and sales rose 16.3%**, both significant at the 1% level.
Cart value fell 4.5%, which was not significant, and return rates did not change.
Combining the chatbot with escalation to a person raised sales 25% over no service, and the authors estimate that combination's gain over human-only service at about 11.5%, significant only at the 10% level.
For a small store that already answers chat or email within a few hours, the human-only comparison is the fairer one.
The result measures the value of answering a pre-sale question at once for a shopper who has already asked one.
It does not measure the value of showing a chat widget to every visitor, and it has no seller-level analysis.
Two other arms of the same study bear on the comparison later in this report: AI-refined search queries raised sales 2.9%, and AI-written product descriptions raised sales 2.1% and cut returns 3.9%.
A vendor report also summarises an Etsy experiment on AI-driven recommendations in which the conversion change of 0.22% was not significant, though the researchers could not locate Etsy's original source ([MarTech Edge](https://martechedge.com/news/lyro-report-examines-the-rise-of-ai-shopping-assistants)).

### Vendor figures compare shoppers who chat with shoppers who do not

Every other figure is attribution on self-selected shoppers.
Gorgias reports that its shopping assistant influences 1.9% of web revenue on enabled accounts and 3% to 4% in considered-purchase categories such as vehicles and parts, furniture and electronics (vendor claim, June 2026) ([Gorgias](https://www.gorgias.com/research/ai-shopping-assistants-are-now-officially-influencing-sales)).
Gorgias counts an order as influenced when the shopper talked to the assistant within three days, says about 80% of the influenced revenue rests on that weakest signal, and itself calls the figures associative and not causal.
Amazon says shoppers who use its assistant are about 60% more likely to complete a purchase, and Walmart says its assistant's users have order values about 35% higher, both without a published method (vendor claims via press reports) ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/amazon-says-its-ai-shopping-assistant-is-gaining-traction-with-rufus-users-up-115/); [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/walmart-says-ai-users-build-35-bigger-baskets-than-others/)).
In the controlled data the gain came through conversion and basket size did not move, so the higher order values in vendor reports most likely reflect which shoppers choose to chat.
No vendor has published a completed holdout test of an on-site shopping assistant, and the researchers' own searches for one from Amazon, Shopify, Gorgias, Rep AI, Tidio or Alhena found only methodology guides ([Alhena](https://alhena.ai/blog/measure-incremental-revenue-ai-holdout-test/)).
Rep AI says it holds back a share of sessions for measurement and publishes neither the holdout size nor the results (competitor blog) ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/rep-ai-review)).
Support deflection figures have the same weakness.
One vendor's benchmark reports a verified median AI resolution rate of 41%, other vendors cite 60% to 80%, and no independent audit exists ([Aissist](https://aissist.io/industries/ai-customer-service-benchmark); [Fin](https://fin.ai/learn/ai-resolution-rate)).

### Fewer than 1 in 100 visits becomes a chat

The largest dataset found counted 40.1 million chat conversations against 4.78 billion website visits across 175,438 accounts in 2024, or **0.84% of visits** (vendor data) ([Smartsupp](https://www.smartsupp.com/blog/analysing-5-billion-website-visits-how-ecommerce-customers-use-chat/)).
Alhena claims roughly 1% engagement for a floating widget and 6% or more for brands that use three placements, and its own figures are inconsistent with each other (vendor claims, search summary only) ([Alhena](https://alhena.ai/blog/chat-widget-design-ecommerce-conversions/)).
Sources mix opening the widget, sending a message and holding a conversation, so the rates are not directly comparable.
The researchers' planning figure for a passive widget on a small store is 0.5% to 1% of sessions, and rates of 3% to 6% are vendor-claimed upper values that depend on proactive prompts.
Low engagement, more than answer quality, caps the revenue a small store can get from an assistant.

### Small-store payback is about $80 to $330 a month on measured rates

No primary dataset gives traffic or chat counts by store revenue band, so the table below is the researchers' arithmetic.
It assumes a 1.4% conversion rate (vendor benchmark of about 420 stores, seen in secondary blogs), a $90 average order (blog figures that could not be traced to a primary source), 0.84 chats per 100 sessions (vendor data), half of chats being pre-sale, and the marketplace trial's lift of 1.31 points applied to each pre-sale chat.
The last assumption is generous, because that lift was measured against no answer at all.

| Yearly revenue | Monthly sessions | Monthly chats | Added revenue at 0.84% engagement | Added revenue at 5% engagement | Gorgias's 1.9% treated as fully incremental |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $250,000 | 16,500 | 139 | About $80 | About $490 | About $400 |
| $1,000,000 | 66,100 | 556 | About $330 | About $1,950 | About $1,580 |

All cells are researcher estimates from the stated assumptions, with inputs from [Red Stag Fulfillment](https://redstagfulfillment.com/average-conversion-rate-for-shopify-stores/), [Smartsupp](https://www.smartsupp.com/blog/analysing-5-billion-website-visits-how-ecommerce-customers-use-chat/), [arXiv 2510.12049](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12049v5) and [Gorgias](https://www.gorgias.com/research/ai-shopping-assistants-are-now-officially-influencing-sales).

These are revenue figures before product margin.
On the support side, a 41% resolution rate would remove 19 to 48 tickets a month at $250,000 in revenue, or roughly 2 to 8 hours of work.
Against prices of about $50 to $130 a month at 10,000 to 16,000 sessions, a store under about $250,000 is unlikely to recover the cost on measured evidence unless its order value is high.
Near $1 million the case is borderline at measured engagement and positive only if engagement is several times the benchmark.
High-ticket stores are the exception, since one saved order of a few hundred dollars covers a month's fee.
Small stores also cannot check the claim themselves.
A vendor rule of thumb says a holdout test needs 15,000 to 20,000 visitors per group to detect a 5% lift, or about 150,000 sessions with a 90/10 split (vendor guidance) ([Alhena](https://alhena.ai/blog/measure-incremental-revenue-ai-holdout-test/)).
No store under $1 million has that traffic in a month, so small merchants take the vendor's dashboard on trust.

## A fine-tuned model is not the advantage, and inference is no longer the cost

### Taobao's quality rests on data that one store does not own

Alibaba launched the Qwen shopping assistant inside the Taobao app on 11 May 2026 and connected it to a catalog it puts at more than 4 billion products (vendor claim) ([Alibaba Cloud](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/alibaba-opens-all-of-taobao-to-qwen-ai-ushering-in-a-new-agentic-shopping-experience_603104)).
Alibaba attributes the assistant's quality to that catalog, billions of customer reviews, order history, a skills library for orders, logistics and after-sales service, and a model refined on more than two decades of transaction data.
The announcement gives no sales, conversion or latency figure, and no technical paper describes the system.
Amazon trained a custom shopping model on its whole catalog, reviews and community questions in 2024 and, according to a later engineering post the researchers saw only in summary, now routes between that model, Claude Sonnet and Amazon Nova ([Amazon Science](https://amazon.science/blog/the-technology-behind-amazons-genai-powered-shopping-assistant-rufus); [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/how-rufus-scales-conversational-shopping-experiences-to-millions-of-amazon-customers-with-amazon-bedrock)).
Zalando's size advice draws on sizing data from 53 million customers (vendor claim via press, summary only) ([Modaes](https://www.modaes.com/global/companies/zalando-is-refining-its-ai-assistant-using-sizing-data-from-53-million-customers)).
The company with the most shopping data did not find one domain model sufficient.

The features that make these assistants stand out depend on data and account systems a single store lacks.
Review synthesis across many buyers, personalisation from years of purchases, size prediction, cross-seller price comparison, coupon stacking and price tracking with automatic ordering all fall in that group.
What one small store can have is grounded answers about its own products and policies, comparison inside its own range, clarifying questions, cart building, order status and handoff to a person.
Platform agents and open starter code now offer every item on that second list.
A small catalog of tens to a few thousand items makes retrieval easy.
The hard problems are missing or messy attributes, thin product copy and stale price and stock.
On the ShoppingBench benchmark the largest error class for a frontier model was a mismatch between the request and product attributes (independent study, August 2025) ([arXiv 2508.04266](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.04266v3)).
A vendor serving many stores could build a cross-store asset of attribute schemas and conversation traces, which is the only route to something like marketplace data, and it raises consent questions with every merchant.

### Tuned models beat their own base and trail the current frontier

The research from 2023 to 2026 shows one consistent pattern.
A tuned model beats its own untuned base by a wide margin on the task it was trained for, and matches or narrowly beats a frontier model of the previous generation on that task.
No study in the notes shows a tuned domain model beating the current frontier across shopping tasks, and none compares a tuned small model with the November 2026 frontier.

| Study | Result for tuning | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Shopping MMLU, 57 tasks on Amazon data (vendor-authored academic benchmark, 2024) | Tuned 7B and 13B models beat their bases by 7 to 16 points per skill | The best model was a general proprietary one, tuning did not help a base under 5B, and shopping skill tracked general ability with correlations of 0.9 or more |
| KDD Cup 2024 winning entry (competition paper) | Tuning a 72B model on about 502,000 examples gained 0.027 to 0.150 per track | The untuned model would have placed 9th, and no proprietary frontier model was allowed |
| ShoppingBench agent benchmark (independent study, August 2025) | A 4B model rose from 18.0% to 48.7% after supervised tuning and reinforcement learning, 0.5 points above GPT-4.1 | Training data was distilled from a stronger model, the test shares its sandbox with training, GPT-4.1 is one to two generations old, and the tuned model trailed on knowledge tasks by 46.7% to 62.0% |
| MMShopBench (arXiv paper, July 2026, abstract only) | Tuning substantially narrows the gap between an open model and proprietary leaders | The gap narrowed and did not close |
| CommerceAgentBench, 107 merchant tasks (vendor benchmark via press, September 2026) | No tuned entrant reported | The best frontier model passed 61.7%, and the harness alone moved the same model by 1.0 to 5.6 points |

Sources for the table, in row order: [arXiv 2410.20745](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20745v2); [arXiv 2408.04658](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04658v1); [arXiv 2508.04266](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.04266v3); [arXiv 2607.29002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29002); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=4207827).

Tuning helps most on narrow, repetitive, labelled work: attribute extraction and normalisation, query rewriting, relevance scoring, behaviour prediction and tool-call sequences for a fixed tool set.
It helps least on open product knowledge, on skills outside the training data, and wherever a new general model release erases the lead and the tuning has to be repeated.
It needs data a new vendor does not have, since the winning competition team used half a million examples and generated a large share of them.
A tuned small model also inherits none of a lab's safety work.
In Microsoft's marketplace simulation, prompt injection in seller content redirected all payments to the manipulating seller for three models, two of them small open models (independent study, November 2025, summary only) ([arXiv 2510.25779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25779)).
The harness result is the most useful one for a two-person team, because it shows that the engineering around the model can matter as much as the choice between a tuned and an untuned model.
At the small-store end, vendors sell conversion lift, automation rates and guarantees, and treat the model as a hidden commodity.
A fine-tuned commerce model is therefore not a recognised buying criterion today.
Fin is the counter-example at scale, with a proprietary model in the rationale for a $3.6 billion deal, and its benchmark claims were not independently verified ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/06/15/salesforce-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-fin/)).
The team's fine-tuning skill would count only if it showed up as fewer wrong answers on a store's own catalog, with proof the merchant can see.

### A typical conversation costs a fifth of a cent to 43 cents in model fees

The figures in this subsection are the researchers' calculations from official price lists read in November 2026, and they are not published figures.
The assumed typical conversation has 4 shopper turns, each with one tool call and one answer, a 3,000-token system prompt with tool definitions, 1,200 tokens per trimmed tool result and 250 tokens per answer.
That comes to about 47,960 input tokens and 1,240 output tokens, of which about 39,010 input tokens are cache reads when prompt caching is on.
The heavy conversation assumes about 200,000 input tokens and 3,000 output tokens.
No published average for tokens per store-chat conversation exists, so both lengths are assumptions.

| Model tier | Example models | Typical, no caching | Typical, with caching | Heavy, with caching |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Top | Claude Fable 5.2, GPT-6.2 Astra | $0.43 | $0.14 to $0.15 | $0.55 |
| Upper mid | Claude Opus 5.5 | $0.22 | $0.08 | $0.29 |
| Mid | Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.2 Sol | $0.11 | $0.03 to $0.04 | $0.13 to $0.15 |
| Lower mid | GPT-6.1 Terra | $0.05 | $0.02 | $0.06 |
| Small, from a lab | Claude Haiku 5.5, GPT-6 Luna | $0.005 | $0.002 | $0.007 to $0.008 |
| Small open model, hosted | Qwen3.5 9B at April 2026 prices | $0.002 to $0.005 | No caching assumed | About $0.009 without caching |

Price inputs: [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing) and [DeepInfra](https://deepinfra.com/blog/qwen3-5-9b-api-benchmarks) (the last is a vendor blog from April 2026 and its prices were not rechecked).

Three conclusions follow from these estimates.
First, the labs' cheapest models cost about the same per token as a hosted small open model, so fine-tuning or self-hosting saves a fraction of a cent per conversation.
One always-on L4 GPU costs about $317 to $576 a month, and $317 buys 63,000 to 144,000 typical conversations on a hosted small model, so a dedicated GPU starts to pay only above roughly 60,000 to 140,000 conversations a month across all stores, before engineering time and redundancy ([CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/cloud-gpu-pricing/)).
Second, model cost is small against a $20 to $100 monthly plan only when a small or mid-tier model carries most traffic.
The margin figures assume 200, 1,000 or 5,000 conversations per store per month, and those volumes are also assumptions.
A $50 plan keeps about 90% on a small model and about 20% on a mid-tier model at 1,000 conversations a month, and it loses money on a top model.
A router that sends easy turns to a small model and hard turns to a mid-tier one keeps blended cost near $0.01 to $0.02 per conversation.
These margins exclude hosting, search infrastructure, payment fees, support and abusive conversations.
Third, incumbents charge about $0.90 to $2.00 per resolved conversation, which is 20 to 400 times the model cost of a typical conversation on small or mid-tier models (vendor price lists compiled by an agency index, summary only) ([Elogic](https://elogic.co/?p=475505)).
Value, integration and support set the price of these products, and inference does not.
That removes cost as a place for a fine-tuning team to win.
Tuning is cheap where it is useful.
By the researchers' estimate, enriching 5,000 products through a lab's small model in batch mode costs about $0.75.

## Self-hosted stores selling considered purchases are underserved and hard to reach

Store Leads counted about 14.2 million live stores on 20 November 2026, with WooCommerce at 4.32 million, Shopify at 3.35 million and a catch-all Custom Cart class at 2.19 million (independent data vendor, crawl-based) ([Store Leads](https://storeleads.app/reports)).
These counts move a lot between snapshots and should be read as approximate.
Nearly all stores are small.
Shopify Plus domains are 2.5% of Shopify stores, and about half of Shopify and WooCommerce stores with a product count list fewer than 25 products ([Store Leads](https://storeleads.app/reports/shopify)).
Catalog size is a better proxy for need than revenue, and by the researchers' sums about 888,000 Shopify stores and about 975,000 WooCommerce stores list 100 or more products.

WooCommerce and self-hosted stores are the clearest structural gap.
WooCommerce is the largest platform, has no native shopper-facing assistant, and over-indexes on categories where vendor data shows the most assistant influence: 347,730 stores in business and industrial, 147,828 in computers and 134,563 in autos and vehicles ([Store Leads](https://storeleads.app/reports/WooCommerce)).
Its existing assistants are plugins on $49 to $199 one-time or yearly licences that need the merchant's own API key, or hosted tools whose store features start at $95 a month, according to a comparison written by a plugin seller ([PureThemes](https://purethemes.net/best-ai-chatbot-for-woocommerce-comparison/)).
The gap comes with costs.
The WooCommerce base fell 10% year over year in the third quarter of 2026, its merchants are used to licence prices an order of magnitude below hosted software, it has no app store channel that can be measured, and hosting varies widely.
Only 12.3% of WooCommerce stores are in the US, with 91,000 to 141,000 each in Germany, Italy, France, Spain, the Netherlands and Poland.
The notes contain no data on what WooCommerce merchants spend on tools and no test of assistant quality in any non-English language.

The other candidate segments are weaker.
Stores paying $200 or more a month are mid-sized Shopify stores at roughly $1 million to $20 million in revenue, a pool the researchers put in the tens of thousands, and their main pain is a billing unit that scales with traffic and not with sales.
India has about 358,000 tracked stores and a crowded WhatsApp assistant market where AI add-ons sell for roughly $30 to $75 a month, with one vendor charging India about a quarter of its international price (independent data vendor for the store count, software directory review for the prices) ([Store Leads](https://storeleads.app/reports); [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/tools/ai-agents/best-ai-agents-for-whatsapp-2026)).
Southeast Asia's online commerce runs mostly through marketplaces and social platforms, with Shopee and TikTok Shop holding about 54% and 38% of Indonesia's ecommerce in one industry study (press report, search summary only) ([KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/tiktok-sea-alibaba-owned-e-commerce-corner-asean-markets)).
Latin America and Saudi Arabia are served by the dominant local platforms themselves.

Distribution is the constraint that applies to every segment.
The Shopify App Store had about 22,500 live apps in June 2026, 51% of them with zero reviews, and an estimated 1% of apps take 72% of revenue (app vendor analysis and estimate) ([RevenueHunt](https://revenuehunt.com/state-of-the-shopify-app-economy/)).
A second model puts the youngest fifth of apps at about $190 a month in revenue and finds that a typical app loses about four shops for every ten it gains in a year (plugin vendor estimate with a stated error of up to a factor of two) ([Barn2](https://barn2.com/blog/shopify-app-store-saturation/)).
No dollar figure for customer acquisition cost was found.
With entry prices near $10 to $50 and that churn, lifetime revenue per small merchant is too low to pay for acquisition.

## Apparent gaps that are not real opportunities

A cheaper assistant on Shopify is not an opportunity.
The floor is zero, long-tail apps start at $9.99 or offer 100 free conversations, and half the app store has no reviews.

Taobao-level quality for a single small store is not an opportunity.
The features that define it depend on cross-buyer reviews, long purchase histories, sizing data and a shared wallet, and the one route to similar data, pooling across many stores, needs a customer base the team does not have.

A fine-tuned model as the selling point is not an opportunity.
Small merchants do not buy on the model, no study shows a tuned model beating the current frontier across shopping tasks, and each general model release erodes the lead.

Self-hosting to cut cost is not an opportunity below roughly 60,000 to 140,000 conversations a month in total, which is a researcher estimate.

Support deflection for stores under about $250,000 in revenue is not an opportunity.
The estimated saving is 2 to 8 hours of work a month.

A share-of-attributed-sales price is not an evident opportunity.
The one on-site vendor found using it is delisted for unknown reasons, attribution is the number merchants cannot check, and the notes hold no evidence either way on whether merchants would prefer it.

Getting a Shopify store into ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot is not an opportunity.
Shopify does it by default at no extra reported fee.

A WhatsApp assistant for India is not an opportunity for this team on the evidence in the notes.
A dozen funded providers sell one at $30 to $75 a month, Meta sells its own, and Meta now charges for the replies third-party bots send.
Meta's own table shows a cheap third-party model still undercutting its agent in Brazil, at about $27 against $40 to $50 per 1,000 messages, but Meta sets and can change those rates ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/non-template-messages/)).

Latin America on Tiendanube and Saudi Arabia on Salla are not opportunities, because the platform owner ships the assistant.
An on-site assistant for Southeast Asia addresses a minority of sellers.

Voice and image input are not reasons a small merchant buys.
The notes found no measured demand on small stores, and the one strong signal is voice on an Indian marketplace, reported by the company itself (vendor claim, search summary only) ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/03/meesho-launches-ai-shopping-assistant-vaani)).

Guided-selling quizzes as a standalone Shopify category are small.
The three category leaders have 422, 193 and 171 reviews (analysis by a vendor that competes in the category) ([RevenueHunt](https://revenuehunt.com/state-of-the-shopify-app-economy/)).

A holdout dashboard for the smallest stores is not buildable as promised.
Their traffic cannot detect a 5% lift in a month, so proof for them has to take another form, such as a replay against their own past questions.

## The first idea has the stronger case, and the assistant's useful parts fit inside it

### The two ideas side by side

The team's first idea is a product for sites whose owners control their own code.
It connects the customer's GitHub repo, Search Console, analytics and ad accounts, finds search, AI-search visibility, ad and conversion problems, ships each fix as a small pull request, checks the fix on the live site, and keeps a record of every change with before-and-after results.
The earlier research behind it found that buyers pay $95 to $495 a month for trackers that measure and do not fix, with Peec's three plans as the reference prices, or $2,500 to $7,500 a month for agencies, with one agency's rate card starting at $2,500 for 4 articles a month (vendor pricing pages) ([Peec](https://peec.ai/pricing); [Red-engage](https://red-engage.com/blog/geo-agency-pricing)).
It found almost no product that both does the work on the customer's own code and proves the result.
Existing pull-request agents handle head tags and schema, stop at the open pull request, and show little traction, and Ahrefs distributes one inside a $99 a month workspace (vendor docs) ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/agent-a)).

| Question | On-site assistant for small stores | Repo-connected product that fixes and proves |
|---|---|---|
| Does a platform give the core away? | Yes on Shopify, BigCommerce and Wix, all within 2026 | Partly: free consoles and hosted-CMS fixes exist, and nothing fixes code-based sites outside those platforms |
| Direct competition | At least a dozen funded vendors and a long tail at $9.99 | A handful of pull-request agents at $29 to $199 with thin traction, plus Ahrefs at $99 |
| Evidence that buyers feel the pain | First-page low-star reviews and small forum threads, with no survey and no Reddit | Complaints counted across 8 to 12 independent sources each, and vendor-run surveys in which attribution is the top problem |
| Evidence that the product's effect is real | One randomized trial on a marketplace, and an estimated $80 to $330 a month for small stores | Fixes with measured support are few (crawler access and server-rendered content), most on-page tactics test as neutral, and AI referrals were 0.32% of visits in one vendor study |
| What the buyer already spends | $50 to $300 a month on all apps under $1 million in revenue (model), and 65,441 Shopify stores above $100 | A third of surveyed companies spend $500 to $2,000 a month on this work, with company size not reported |
| Use of the team's skills | Fine-tuning is neither a buying criterion nor a cost edge | Repo access and engineering depth are the product, and fine-tuning is not central |
| Distribution | A saturated app store in which a typical app loses four shops for every ten gained | Unmeasured, and developer-led teams have no proven channel |
| Largest unknown | Whether any small-store segment pays for quality over a free default | Whether developer-led teams grant repo access and pay more than a tracker costs |

Sources for the right-hand column: [Minuttia](https://minuttia.com/state-of-aeo/), [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/ai-traffic-research-study/) and [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/geo-vs-seo-ppc-survey-report), all vendor-run or vendor-published.
Sources for the left-hand column appear in the sections above.

The two markets have the same shape.
In both, buyers pay for an output they cannot verify, a visibility score in one and an influenced-revenue dashboard in the other, and in both the scarce thing is proof.
The difference is what stands between a new product and the buyer.
In the assistant market the platform owner ships a free default with identity data no outsider can match, and it did so within about a year of opening the interfaces that third parties built on.
In the first idea's market the free features stop at the boundary of hosted platforms, and code-based sites sit outside it.
The newer notes also update one point from the earlier research.
The earlier report cited a peer-reviewed study, on data ending in mid-2025, in which ChatGPT referrals converted worse than most channels ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/llms-google-referral-conversion-study-463747)).
Adobe's vendor data agrees for March 2025 and shows the sign reversed by March 2026, so AI referral traffic remains small and now appears to convert better than other traffic on large US retail sites.

### The evidence favours the first idea, with the same unanswered pricing question

The evidence supports building the first idea over the assistant.
The support is moderate and not strong.
It is strongest on two structural facts: no platform gives the first idea's core away to code-based sites, and its buyers have documented complaints and existing budgets at two price points.
It is weak in the same place as the assistant's case, since neither body of research measures what a buyer would pay for the product itself.
The evidence does not support a standalone assistant for small stores.
The mass-market version competes with a free default, the measurable payback at small-store traffic is below most prices, the team's fine-tuning skill buys neither quality that buyers can see nor a cost advantage, and the one underserved segment is shrinking and has no channel.
A two-person team would also take on legal exposure for what the assistant says and yearly platform migrations.

### A combination makes sense in one direction only

The combination the evidence supports is the first product absorbing commerce-specific work for stores that control their own code.
It does not support a chat product that later grows a fixing feature.
The two customer sets overlap only in part.
Most small stores sit on hosted platforms with no repo to connect, so the overlap is custom-built and headless stores and developer-run self-hosted stores.
Store Leads' Custom Cart class of 2.19 million is a catch-all, the notes found no count of headless stores, and they do not say how many self-hosted stores keep their code where a product could connect to it.
Four forms follow, in order of how well the evidence supports them.

The first form is product-data and pre-sale content fixes shipped as pull requests.
The assistant research shows that the scarce input for any shopping assistant is structured, complete product data, and that the same data feeds on-site search, the platform's own agent and external AI agents.
In the marketplace trial, AI-written product descriptions raised sales 2.1% and cut returns 3.9%, and refined search queries raised sales 2.9%, and those changes reach every visitor where a chat reaches fewer than 1 in 100 ([arXiv 2510.12049](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12049v5)).
Shopify claims that AI searches powered by its structured catalog convert at twice the rate of those using scraped data (vendor claim) ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/news/spring-26-edition-merchant)).
The earlier research found that stores outside Shopify must apply to share a product feed with ChatGPT and that Shopify's per-channel AI reports are unavailable to headless stores (vendor pages) ([ChatGPT merchants page](https://chatgpt.com/merchants/); [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/ca/blog/how-agentic-commerce-works)).
This is the first idea's conversion and AI-visibility work applied to a store, and it is also where fine-tuning has its best published support.
The caveat is scale: the trial's effects were measured on a very large marketplace, and a small store cannot detect a 2% lift.

The second form is measurement.
The first idea's change record, extended to on-site conversion changes and run as a holdout where traffic allows, occupies the position that no assistant vendor holds in public.
It has to be sized to the customer's traffic, with a before-and-after comparison and intervals for sites that cannot support a holdout.

The third form is fine-tuning as internal tooling.
The published gains sit in offline attribute extraction and normalisation, query classification and routing, at a cost the researchers estimate in cents per catalog.
A sensible sequence is to build a store-level evaluation set first, measure a frontier model, a lab's small model and an untuned open model on it, and tune only where a gap remains.
The tuned model is then a cost and quality detail inside the product and not a claim made to buyers.

The fourth form is conditional and comes later: a pre-sale answer component delivered as code into the customer's own storefront.
It would suit code-owning stores in considered-purchase categories with enough traffic to measure, where no platform default exists.
The evidence points to its shape: placed in the search box or product page as Forrester advises, limited to the pre-purchase tasks shoppers say they prefer AI for, with prices and policies read from the system of record, a visible route to a person, and a holdout built in.
Its conversation log would also show which shopper questions the site fails to answer, which the fixing product can then address at the source.
Model cost is not an obstacle at about $0.005 to $0.04 per typical conversation on the researchers' estimate.
Demand for this component is unmeasured, the size of the eligible segment is unknown, and it adds liability and support load, so the evidence supports testing it with existing customers and does not support building it first.

The forms the evidence argues against are a Shopify App Store chat widget, a WhatsApp agent, a fine-tuned chat model marketed as such, and any assistant aimed at stores under about $250,000 in revenue.

## Conclusion

The two research efforts answer different questions and reach one finding: buyers in both markets are sold a number they cannot check, and the product that is missing is the one that does the work and shows what changed.
The assistant market adds a warning the earlier research could only infer.
A platform that opens an interface can ship its own free version within a year, as Shopify did between its 2025 agent tools and its June 2026 agent, so the first idea's safe ground is sites that no hosted platform controls, and its proof claims have to be sized to each customer's traffic.
It also redirects the team's fine-tuning skill from the chat window, where it has no demonstrated edge, to the product data underneath, where the published gains are and where one fix serves the store's search, any assistant and outside AI agents at once.

Four things would change this reading.
A published holdout showing a sitewide lift on small stores would reopen the assistant case.
So would evidence that self-hosted merchants in considered-purchase categories pay monthly fees above their plugin-licence habits, a decision by Shopify to cap or charge for its agent, or a neutral benchmark showing the free defaults failing badly on real catalogs.
Each can be checked cheaply before anything is built: a handful of code-owning stores with large catalogs, a set of product-data fixes with before-and-after records, a log of the pre-sale questions those stores receive, and a direct question about price.
Those same records would test the first idea's own open question, which is whether owners who control their code will grant access and pay for proven work.
