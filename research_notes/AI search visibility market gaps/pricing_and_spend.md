# Pricing and spend: what businesses pay for AI search visibility (GEO / AEO)

Research date: 2026-11-22.
Every price marked "observed 2026-11-22" was read from the vendor's own live page on that date.
Prices are USD unless a row says EUR, GBP or INR.
"Secondary" means a comparison post, usually written by a company that sells a competing tool or service, so treat it as lower confidence.
Cost per tracked prompt is my own arithmetic (list monthly price divided by included prompts) unless a source is named for it.

## Tool subscription pricing and plan limits

### Takeaway

Self-serve trackers charge about $1.40 to $2.00 per tracked prompt per month at 3 to 4 engines with daily refresh, which works out to roughly $0.02 per AI response, and entry plans cover only 16 to 50 prompts.
The category leaders are leaving that tier: Profound retired its self-serve plans in September 2026 and lists a $100,000 12-month contract on AWS Marketplace, while Adobe, Conductor, BrightEdge and AirOps publish no prices at all.

### Inferences

- The self-serve market has a common unit price.
  Peec, Promptwatch, Otterly and Writesonic all land between $1.40 and $2.50 per tracked prompt per month, and Peec and Promptwatch have identical $95 and $245 price points for 50 and 150 prompts.
- Semrush charges about twice that per prompt ($3 to $4), and Ahrefs is the cheapest per check ($0.01 to $0.02), because both sell tracking as an attachment to an SEO suite the buyer already pays for.
- Vendors meter in four different units (prompts, credits, AI responses, checks), so a buyer cannot compare plans without doing the arithmetic in the table above.
  AthenaHQ's $295 plan buys 3,600 responses while Scrunch's $250 plan buys 15,000, a 4.9x difference in cost per response.
- Entry tiers of 16 to 50 prompts are thin.
  A business with 5 products and 10 questions per product already exceeds every plan under $100.
- Three price tiers exist: under $100 for 16 to 50 prompts, $200 to $600 for 100 to 400 prompts, and $50K to $300K per year for enterprise contracts (Profound, Peec Enterprise, AirOps, Conductor, BrightEdge).
  The gap between a $495 plan and a $100,000 contract has few published options.
- What a tracking subscription delivers is measurement: mention rate, citations, share of voice and cited sources.
  The buyer still has to pick the prompts, write or fix pages, win off-site mentions and prove revenue impact.
  Plans that include content (Scrunch Core's 10 generated pages, Writesonic's 15 to 50 articles, Promptwatch Professional, AirOps tasks) cover drafting only, and none of the pages reviewed include off-site placement.
- Profound's move to Enterprise-only and Peec's $100K to $300K contracts show the best-funded vendors moving upmarket, which leaves SMB buyers with small-prompt trackers and suite add-ons.

### Gaps

- Adobe LLM Optimizer (Brand Visibility) has no public price, and I could not open Adobe's pages to confirm the 1,000-prompt minimum.
- Conductor, BrightEdge and AirOps prices are third-party estimates only.
- SE Ranking does not publish which of its 75, 200 and 1,000-prompt tiers costs $71.
- Profound's actual contract sizes are unknown beyond the $100,000 AWS list price, and its retired plan prices conflict across sources ($399 and $499 against $99 and $399).
- The name "Semrush One" no longer appears on Semrush's pricing page, which now says "SEO + AI Search" plans, and I found no statement on how the Adobe acquisition changes pricing.
- Reddit and G2 pricing notes were not reachable from the search tool, so there is no user-reported discount or negotiated-price data here.
- Refresh frequency is unstated for AthenaHQ, and several "engines covered" counts mix chat apps with API models.

## Agency and consultant fees

### Takeaway

Published GEO retainers run from about $350 per month for productized small-business plans to $10,000+ per month for full-service agencies, with the most common band at $2,500 to $7,500 per month plus a $1,500 to $3,500 setup fee and a 3 to 6 month minimum.
A mid-tier retainer typically tracks only 25 to 130 prompts and ships 2 to 10 articles and a handful of Reddit posts a month, and no agency reviewed guarantees citations.

- No source reports results per dollar, such as citations gained per $1,000 of retainer.

### Inferences

- Disclosed revenue supports the low end of the market estimates.

- The budget numbers disagree because they measure different bases: 12% of digital budgets (Conductor), 24% of search or content budgets (Fractl), and 40% with no dedicated budget (Minuttia).
  Read together, they suggest most AI search spend is reallocated SEO and content money, not new budget.
- Measurement is the top unmet need in every survey: 40.6% (Minuttia), 80.2% (Gather) and 56.0% (Semify SMBs) name attribution or ROI as the problem, even though 73% have already bought a monitoring tool.
  The tools sold today report visibility, and buyers want proof of revenue.
- Valuation is far ahead of revenue.
  Profound's $1.8B valuation sits beside a competitor at $20M ARR and reported talks near a $200M valuation, which implies investors are pricing in a move from tracking into content agents and marketing automation.

### Gaps

- I found no Gartner, Forrester or IDC estimate of GEO/AEO spend, and no dollar budget figures in any survey.
- No report gives a 2027-specific market figure, so any 2027 number would be an interpolation.
- MarketsandMarkets has a GEO report but its page returned 403.
- Profound's ARR is undisclosed, and I did not access The Information or Fortune coverage that may carry estimates.
- The market reports were read from search snippets only, so their definitions and methods are unverified.
- Minuttia's survey field dates are not stated, and the Growth Memo write-up is paywalled.

## Who buys and who owns the budget

### Takeaway

Buyers split into Fortune 500 brands on six-figure contracts (Profound, AirOps, Peec Enterprise), agencies that resell trackers (about half of Peec's customers), and small teams on $95 to $495 plans.
The SEO team owns AEO at 52.8% of companies and funds it from the SEO budget, with performance marketing and SEO committing more than brand or content, and I found no evidence of comms or PR owning the budget.

### Inferences

- The budget holder is usually the SEO lead, spending from the SEO or content line.
  A CMO-level decision appears only at enterprise scale, where contracts reach six figures and the vendor (Profound, Conductor, Adobe) sells to marketing leadership.
- Brand and comms teams commit the least budget and do not appear as owners in any survey, even though PR agencies and Muck Rack-style data argue that earned media drives most AI citations.
  The team that owns the budget (SEO) is not the team that controls the highest-cited channel (PR), which leaves off-site work under-owned.
- SaaS and technology companies and agencies dominate tool adoption, and financial services shows the highest stated budget share among enterprises.
  Profound's named customers add retail, telecom and beauty.
- Agencies are a distribution channel more than a buyer of last resort: half of Peec's accounts are agencies, yet only 2.7% to 25% of in-house teams say they outsource.
- Buyers choose on proof (case studies and method) and still call the work SEO, so GEO-specific branding carries little weight in a purchase.
- The SMB buyer has the least support.
  Time and expertise are a bigger obstacle than budget, the sub-$100 tools cover 16 to 50 prompts with no execution, and productized agency plans under $1,000 are described by competitors as relabeled SEO.

### Gaps

- No survey found reports ownership by brand, comms or PR teams specifically, or CMO sign-off rates.
- Spend by industry in dollars is not available, only stated budget shares from one vendor survey.
- SMB spend levels are unknown beyond tool list prices, and the one SMB survey has 75 respondents.
- I found no region split of buyers beyond Peec's US revenue share, and no India or EU buyer data.
- Profound's customer mix by industry and size is self-reported and unaudited.
